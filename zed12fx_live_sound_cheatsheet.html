Allen & Heath ZED-12FX

Live Sound Setup Guide — Vocals + Fiddle DI + E. Guitar mic + Bass DI
Before soundcheck — reset the board

Faders

Slide all channel faders all the way down. Master fader and monitor knob stay down too for now.

EQ

Turn all EQ knobs to 12 o'clock (straight up). This means "no change" — flat.

AUX sends

Turn all AUX knobs fully left (counter-clockwise). That's zero — nothing being sent.

FX

FX return fader all the way down. You'll add reverb later, after everything else is set.

AUX routing

Pre-fader AUX 1

This powers your monitor wedge (the speaker on stage that faces the band). It's independent — when you change the main volume for the audience, the monitors don't change.

Pre-fader AUX 2

A second, separate monitor mix — useful if two musicians need to hear different things. If you only have one wedge, leave all AUX 2 knobs at zero.

Post-fader AUX 3

Sends signal to the board's built-in reverb/effects. When you turn the main fader down, this goes down too — so the reverb fades naturally with the instrument.

Full setup walkthrough
1

Patch & prepare

Cables in, phantom power, board zeroed
  • Confirm all faders are down, EQ knobs at 12 o'clock, AUX sends at zero, FX return at zero.
  • Plug vocals mic into Ch 1 XLR. Plug fiddle DI into Ch 2 XLR. Plug guitar amp mic into Ch 3 XLR. Plug bass DI into Ch 4 XLR.
  • If the fiddle or bass DI box needs phantom power, press the 48V button on that channel. Wait 30 seconds for it to stabilise. (Most active DI boxes don't need it — check the DI.)
  • Connect the monitor wedge to the AUX 1 output on the back of the board (TRS or XLR depending on your cable). If you have a second monitor, connect it to AUX 2.
  • Connect main PA speakers to the Main L/R outputs.
  • Plug your headphones into the headphone jack on the front of the board.
Before turning on: Check that the master fader and monitor/AUX master knobs are down. Power on in order: mixer first, then amps/powered speakers. Reverse when powering off.
2

Set gain — one channel at a time with PFL

This is the most important step. Get this right and everything else is easier.
What PFL does on the ZED-12FX: Press the PFL button on a channel to route that channel's pre-fader signal to your headphones and the main meter. This lets you hear and meter the channel in isolation — even with the fader down. The audience hears nothing. Only one PFL at a time. Press it again to deselect.
CH 1 Vocals
  • Press the PFL button on Ch 1. The PFL LED lights up and the main meter now shows Ch 1's level.
  • Ask the singer to sing at their loudest (not a whisper — you need to set gain for peaks).
  • Slowly turn the gain knob clockwise. Watch the meter: you want the signal peaking around 0 dB — hitting yellow, just barely touching red on the loudest notes.
  • If the meter is buried in the red, back the gain off. If it's only in the green, turn it up more.
  • Press PFL again to deselect Ch 1 when done.
Vocal gain tip: Leave a little headroom. Singers almost always get louder during the show than during soundcheck. Better to be a touch low than clipping mid-set.
CH 2 Fiddle (DI)
  • Press PFL on Ch 2.
  • Ask the fiddle player to play their loudest passage.
  • Set the gain knob so the meter peaks around 0 dB (yellow, barely red).
  • DI signals can be hot — if you're already in the red with the gain barely up, check if the DI box has a pad switch and engage it.
  • Deselect PFL when done.
CH 3 Electric guitar (mic on amp)
  • Press PFL on Ch 3.
  • Ask the guitarist to play at their loudest — especially any pedal-boosted parts or lead tones. You need to set gain for their peak volume, not their clean tone.
  • Set gain to peak around 0 dB on the meter.
  • If they use heavy distortion, the signal is already compressed — you'll see less dynamic range on the meter. That's normal.
  • Deselect PFL when done.
CH 4 Bass (DI)
  • Press PFL on Ch 4.
  • Ask the bass player to dig in hard on their heaviest parts.
  • Set gain to peak around 0 dB. Bass eats headroom fast — don't set this too hot.
  • If the DI signal is very hot, use the DI box pad or the ZED's line-level input (TRS jack) instead of the XLR mic input.
  • Deselect PFL when done.
You're done with gain. Do not touch the gain knobs again unless something fundamental changes (different mic, different instrument, battery dying in a DI). From now on, control volume with faders only.
3

Build the monitor mix (AUX 1)

Musicians need to hear themselves before you worry about FOH
How AUX 1 monitors work on the ZED-12FX: AUX 1 is pre-fader by default. Each channel has its own AUX 1 knob. Turning that knob sends signal to the AUX 1 output (your monitor wedge) independently of the channel fader. This means you can build a monitor mix that doesn't change when you adjust the FOH faders during the show.
Set the AUX 1 master level
  • On the master section, find the AUX 1 master knob and set it to about 12 o'clock (unity). This gives you room to go up or down later.
Ask each musician what they need

Go one at a time. Stand near the monitor wedge or have the musician give you hand signals (point up = more, flat palm = good, point down = less).

CH 1 Vocals in monitor
  • Turn the AUX 1 knob on Ch 1 up while the singer sings.
  • The singer almost always wants themselves loudest in the monitor. Start here.
  • Set it to a comfortable level — they should hear themselves clearly but it shouldn't be screaming.
Feedback warning: Vocal monitors are the #1 cause of feedback. If the wedge starts ringing, turn AUX 1 on Ch 1 down immediately. Don't push it — find the level just below where it starts feeding back and stay there.
CH 2 Fiddle in monitor
  • Turn AUX 1 on Ch 2 up. The fiddle player will likely want a decent amount of themselves.
  • Since the fiddle is on a DI (no acoustic volume on stage), they depend on the monitor to hear themselves. Give them enough.
CH 3 Electric guitar in monitor
  • Often not needed or very little. The guitarist has their amp right behind them.
  • Turn AUX 1 on Ch 3 up only if the guitarist specifically asks for it.
CH 4 Bass in monitor
  • Bass players going through a DI sometimes want a bit of themselves in the monitor. Ask them.
  • If they have a bass amp on stage, they may not need any.
  • Turn AUX 1 on Ch 4 up to taste.
Second monitor (AUX 2) — optional
  • If you have a second wedge (e.g., one for the singer, one for the fiddle player), connect it to AUX 2 output and repeat the process using each channel's AUX 2 knobs.
  • If everyone shares one wedge, leave AUX 2 at zero.
4

Build the FOH (front of house) mix

What the audience hears through the PA
Bring up the master fader
  • Push the master L/R fader to 0 (unity). Leave it here — control the mix with channel faders, not the master.
Bring up channels one at a time

Ask the band to play a song together. Bring faders up in this order:

  • Ch 4 — Bass first. Push the fader up until you hear it solidly in the PA. This is your foundation.
  • Ch 3 — Electric guitar. Bring it up to sit with the bass. The amp is already filling the room, so you may need less than you think from the PA.
  • Ch 2 — Fiddle. The DI fiddle has no stage volume, so it needs the PA. Bring it up until it sits clearly on top of the rhythm section.
  • Ch 1 — Vocals last. Bring the vocal fader up until the voice sits on top of everything. Vocals should always be the most audible element.
Where should the faders be? There's no exact number. Most faders will end up somewhere between -10 and 0 (unity). If a fader is pushed way past 0 to be heard, the gain was probably set too low — go back to Phase 2 and re-PFL that channel.
Check the main meter
  • With all faders up and the band playing, the main L/R meter should average around 0 dB.
  • Peaks should hit no higher than +3 to +6 dB. If you're constantly in the red, pull all faders down proportionally.
5

EQ each channel

Cut problems first, boost only if needed
ZED-12FX EQ layout (mono channels): Each channel has HF (high frequency, shelving), Mid (sweepable — turn the frequency knob to choose where, then cut/boost), and LF (low frequency, shelving). There's also a HPF button (high-pass filter, ~80 Hz) that cuts everything below ~80 Hz with one press.
CH 1 Vocals EQ
  • Press the HPF button — always on for vocals. Cuts low-end rumble, handling noise, and stage vibration.
  • LF: If the voice sounds muddy or boomy, cut a little (turn counter-clockwise).
  • Mid: Sweep the frequency knob to the 200–400 Hz range. If you hear boxiness or honk, cut slightly. Then sweep to 3–5 kHz — a gentle boost here adds presence and clarity.
  • HF: A small boost adds "air" and sibilance. Leave flat if the mic is already bright (e.g., SM58 doesn't usually need HF boost).
PFL to check EQ: Press PFL on Ch 1 while adjusting EQ. Listen in your headphones to hear exactly what you're changing without the PA colouring it. Deselect PFL when done to hear the full mix again.
CH 2 Fiddle EQ
  • Press HPF — on, but listen carefully. If it thins out the fiddle's body too much, you may leave it off. On the ZED-12FX the HPF is around 80 Hz, which is usually safe for fiddle.
  • Mid: This is the critical one. Sweep to 2–4 kHz and cut. DI fiddle is almost always scratchy and harsh in this range. This single cut makes the biggest difference.
  • LF: If the fiddle sounds thin after the mid cut, add a small LF boost to bring back body (200–250 Hz range).
  • HF: Leave flat or cut slightly if there's too much string noise.
CH 3 Electric guitar EQ
  • Press HPF — on. Cuts low-end rumble from the amp and mic.
  • Mid: Sweep to ~300 Hz. If the guitar sounds boxy or muddy, cut here.
  • Then sweep to 2–5 kHz and cut slightly. This clears space for the fiddle's presence range. The guitar doesn't need to dominate here — it has its amp volume to fill the room.
  • LF: Usually leave flat. The guitar's low-mid grind (100–500 Hz) is its character.
  • HF: Leave flat unless there's excessive hiss or string squeak.
CH 4 Bass EQ
  • HPF: OFF. Bass needs its low end — don't cut it.
  • Mid: Sweep to 300–500 Hz and cut if the bass sounds boxy or muddy. This is the most common bass fix.
  • LF: A small boost at ~80 Hz adds warmth and thump. Don't overdo it — too much low end eats headroom and muddies the whole mix.
  • HF: Usually leave flat. A touch of boost can bring out finger/pick attack if the bass sounds too dull.
6

Add FX (reverb)

Via AUX 3 — subtle is the goal
How FX works on the ZED-12FX: AUX 3 is post-fader and feeds the internal FX processor. Each channel's AUX 3 knob controls how much of that channel goes to the FX. The FX return fader controls how much of the wet (effected) signal comes back into the main mix.
Choose an FX preset
  • Use the FX select knob to choose a hall or plate reverb. For live sound, a medium hall works well. Avoid long/cathedral reverbs — they smear everything.
Send vocals to FX
  • Turn the AUX 3 knob on Ch 1 (vocals) up to about 10–11 o'clock. This sends some vocal signal to the reverb.
  • Bring the FX return fader up until you hear a subtle reverb on the voice. You should feel it more than hear it — if you can obviously hear the reverb, it's probably too much.
Send fiddle to FX
  • Turn AUX 3 on Ch 2 (fiddle) up slightly. Reverb helps a DI fiddle sound less "direct" and more natural/acoustic.
  • Use less than the vocal — try 9–10 o'clock.
Guitar and bass — usually no FX
  • Leave AUX 3 on Ch 3 and Ch 4 at zero. The guitarist likely has their own reverb/effects pedals. Bass rarely benefits from reverb — it muddies the low end.
7

Final check before the show

Walk the room, listen, verify
  • Ask the band to play a full song at show volume.
  • Walk the room — listen from different spots. The mix may sound different at the back vs. the front.
  • Check: can you hear the vocals clearly over everything? If not, don't just push the vocal fader — pull competing instruments down slightly or cut them at 3–5 kHz.
  • Check the main meter: averaging around 0 dB, peaks no higher than +3 to +6.
  • Mute any unused channels — open channels pick up stage noise and reduce headroom.
  • Confirm monitor levels with each musician one last time. Thumbs up from everyone means you're ready.
PFL during the show
  • If something sounds wrong mid-show, press PFL on the suspect channel and listen in your headphones. You'll hear that channel in isolation and can diagnose the problem without the audience hearing you make changes.
  • Make small moves. A 2–3 dB fader adjustment is usually enough. Big moves are jarring.
  • If a channel is unused between songs (e.g., fiddle player takes a break), mute it.
Channel EQ guide

Electric guitar (mic on amp)

HPF:ON cuts rumble/mud
~300 Hz:Cut if boxy or muddy
~2-5 kHz:Cut slightly — leave this range for fiddle
Monitor:Guitarist may not need much — they have their amp

Bass guitar (DI)

HPF:OFF bass needs low end
~300-500 Hz:Cut to reduce boxy mud
~80 Hz:Small boost for warmth
Gain:Watch bass eats headroom fast

Vocals (mic)

HPF:ON always
~200-300 Hz:Cut if muddy or honky
~3-5 kHz:Gentle boost for presence
FX:Light reverb via AUX 3 — subtle
Feedback:Most at risk conservative gain

Fiddle (DI)

HPF:ON but conservative — keep body
~2-4 kHz:Cut harshness — DI fiddle is scratchy here
~200-250 Hz:Small boost if it sounds thin
FX:Reverb helps DI sound natural
Monitor:Fiddle player needs themselves up — acoustic feel
Frequency separation — guitar vs fiddle

Guitar owns: low-mid grind — 100-500 Hz. Cut guitar slightly in the 2-5 kHz presence range.

Fiddle owns: upper-mid sparkle — 1-5 kHz. Give it air, cut slightly in guitar's low-mid range.

Small room (vocals + fiddle only)
  • Only 2 channels — get gain right before anything else
  • PA is just lifting voice and fiddle above the room — not carrying the band
  • Ask the band to turn amps down — don't fight them with the PA
  • Vocal: clarity at 3-5 kHz, cut a little at 800 Hz-2 kHz
  • Fiddle: body at 800 Hz-2 kHz, cut a little at 3-5 kHz
  • Monitor mix is simple — they can likely hear amps naturally
During the show

Feedback (screech/ring)

Don't panic. Pull the channel fader down immediately. Then figure out which channel it was, and turn the AUX 1 (monitor) send down slightly on that channel. The usual culprits: vocal mic or fiddle.

Making adjustments

Small moves only. Nudge faders a tiny bit — the audience notices big jumps. If something sounds wrong, press PFL on that channel and listen in your headphones to figure out the problem privately.

Can't hear the vocals?

Don't just push the vocal fader up. Instead, pull the guitar and fiddle faders down a touch. Or cut their EQ slightly in the 3-5 kHz range. Making space is better than making everything louder.

Glossary — terms used in this guide
Gain:Controls how much the mic/DI signal is amplified at the input. Set once, then don't touch it.
Fader:The sliding volume control for each channel. This is what you move during the show.
PFL:"Pre-Fader Listen." A button that sends that channel to your headphones so you can hear it alone. The audience doesn't hear it.
Unity (0 dB):The "default" position for a fader — marked "0" on the board. Not adding or removing level.
FOH:"Front of House" — what the audience hears through the main PA speakers.
AUX send:A separate output on the board. You use it to send a custom mix to monitors or FX.
DI box:A small box that converts an instrument's signal (bass, fiddle pickup) into a balanced signal the mixer can use via XLR.
HPF:"High-Pass Filter" — a button that cuts deep rumble below ~80 Hz. Cleans up most signals.
EQ:Knobs that let you boost or cut specific frequency ranges (bass/mid/treble) to shape the tone.
Pre-fader:The signal is tapped before the fader — moving the fader doesn't change this send. Used for monitors.
Post-fader:The signal is tapped after the fader — if you turn the fader down, this send goes down too. Used for FX.
Phantom (48V):Power sent through the XLR cable to devices that need it (some DI boxes, condenser mics). Only turn on if needed.
Feedback:That awful loud screech/ring. Happens when a mic picks up its own amplified sound from a speaker in a loop.
Clipping:When the signal is too loud for the board to handle cleanly. The meter goes into the red and it sounds distorted.
